<h1><strong>What Should I Do If My Computer Runs Out of Storage?</strong></h1>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When your computer runs out of storage, start by finding what's eating the space, then clear temporary files, remove apps and big files you don't need, and move the rest to the cloud or an external drive.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If cleanup doesn't free enough room, the next move is extending your partition or upgrading to a larger drive.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This guide walks through each fix in the order that works best, for both Windows and Mac.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You'll also learn what not to delete, how to fix a drive that still looks full after cleanup, and how to keep the problem from coming back.</span></p>
<h2><strong>The Quick Answer: 7 Steps in Order</strong></h2>
<ol>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Check what's using your storage</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with the built-in storage tools.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Clear the easy stuff:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Recycle Bin or Trash, temporary files, and old downloads.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Uninstall apps and delete large files</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> you no longer use.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Move files to the cloud</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> so they stay accessible without filling your drive.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Clean up system leftovers</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> such as old Windows installs or iPhone backups.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Offload files to an external drive.</strong></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Extend your partition or upgrade your drive</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> if the space is still too tight.</span></li>
</ol>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Back up anything important before you start deleting or changing drives. A quick copy to an external disk or cloud service takes minutes and can save you from an expensive mistake.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Signs Your Computer Is Almost Out of Space</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Low storage shows up in a few common ways: a Low Disk Space warning, updates that fail to install, files that won't save, and a computer that feels slow because it has no room to work.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft notes that Windows shows notifications when your drive is running out of free space, so don't ignore those prompts.</span></p>

<h2><strong>Step 2: Clear the Easy Stuff First</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Start with items that carry little risk:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Empty the Recycle Bin or Trash.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Deleted files keep using space until you empty it.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Clear your Downloads folder.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Installers and old PDFs pile up fast.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Turn on Storage Sense (Windows).</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> According to</span><a href="https://support.microsoft.com/windows/654f6ada-7bfc-45e5-966b-e24aded96ad5"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft's Storage Sense guide</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the tool deletes temporary files and Recycle Bin items automatically, and you choose how often it runs.&nbsp;</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Storage Sense is off by default, though Windows may switch it on when your space gets low.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">One catch: Storage Sense works on your system drive only, usually C:. To clean other drives, go to Settings &gt; System &gt; Storage &gt; Advanced storage settings &gt; Storage used on other drives.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Step 3: Uninstall Apps and Delete Large Files</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Apps and big media files often free the most space per minute spent.</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Open your installed apps list and remove anything you haven't touched in months.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Sort your Videos, Downloads, and Documents folders by size, then delete or archive what you don't need.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Remove duplicate photos and old screen recordings.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">On a Mac,</span><a href="https://support.apple.com/ht206996"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Apple's guide to freeing up storage</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> points to built-in utilities that help you find files taking up room so you can delete the ones you no longer need.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Step 4: Move Files to the Cloud</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cloud storage can keep your files available without storing them all on your computer.</span></p>
<p><strong>Windows:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> OneDrive Files On-Demand shows all your files in File Explorer, but online-only files don't take up space on your PC.</span></p>
<p><a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/0e6860d3-d9f3-4971-b321-7092438fb38e"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft explains</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> that you can right-click a file and choose Free up space to make it online only.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That action doesn't delete the file from your OneDrive. The tradeoff: you need an internet connection to open online-only files.</span></p>
<p><strong>Mac:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Apple says macOS can store Desktop and Documents in iCloud Drive, photos and videos in iCloud Photos, and messages and attachments in iCloud.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It can also remove Apple TV movies and shows you've already watched and keep only recent email attachments when space runs low.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Keep in mind that iCloud storage counts against your iCloud plan, so you may need to buy more iCloud space.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Step 5: Clean Up System Leftovers</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This step frees real space, but it needs a little care.</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Windows:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Choose Clean up system files in Disk Cleanup or Cleanup recommendations to remove leftover system data.&nbsp;</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft warns that deleting the Windows.old folder removes your option to go back to your previous version of Windows, so only do it when you're sure you won't roll back.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Mac:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Apple suggests deleting old iPhone or iPad backups stored on your Mac and removing user accounts that no longer need access.&nbsp;</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Apple also describes System Data (or Other) as a general category for files that don't fit elsewhere, so a large number there is worth investigating.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Starting your Mac in safe mode can also temporarily make more space available, according to Apple.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Step 6: Offload Files to an External Drive</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">An external hard drive or SSD works well for photo libraries, old projects, and video archives.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Copy the files over, check that they open from the external drive, and only then delete the originals.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Keep a second copy of anything irreplaceable, because a single external drive can fail like any other.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Step 7: Extend Your Partition or Upgrade Your Drive</strong></h2>

<ul>

<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Windows' built-in Disk Management can extend a volume only into unallocated space next to it, which limits what it can do.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Move to a bigger drive.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Cloning your system to a larger SSD gives you more room without reinstalling Windows.</span></li>
</ul>

<h2><strong>What You Should Not Delete</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When space gets tight, it's tempting to delete anything big. Hold off on these:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Windows system folders</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> such as Windows and Program Files</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Mac system folders</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and library files you don't recognize</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Files you haven't backed up</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and can't replace</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Drivers and apps you still use</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">, even if they look large</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you don't know what a file does, search for it first or leave it alone.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Common Problems After Cleanup</strong></h2>
<p><strong>Your drive still looks full.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Check for large hidden categories, such as System Data on a Mac or old restore points and system files on Windows.</span></p>
<p><strong>You see a Low Disk Space error after cleaning.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Microsoft notes that your Temp folder can quickly fill with Microsoft Store application files, and it recommends resetting or clearing the Microsoft Store cache in that case.</span></p>
<p><strong>Files vanished after using cloud storage.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Online-only files usually remain in your cloud account. Check the cloud folder or sign in on the web before assuming they're gone.</span></p>

<h2><strong>How to Keep Your Storage From Filling Up Again</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Leave Storage Sense on, or use Optimize Storage on your Mac.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Review your Downloads folder once a month.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Move finished projects and large media to an external drive or cloud storage.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Uninstall apps you stop using instead of letting them sit.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Keep some free space available, because your computer needs room for updates and temporary files.</span></li>
</ul>
<h2><strong>Frequently Asked Questions</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>Why does my computer say storage is full when I have few files?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hidden categories like system files, app data, cache, and old backups can take up large amounts of space. Check your storage breakdown to see which category is growing.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Is it safe to use Storage Sense?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Yes. Microsoft designed it to remove temporary files and Recycle Bin items. Review its settings so it only deletes what you're comfortable with.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Does deleting files always free up space?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Not until you empty the Recycle Bin or Trash. Deleted items keep taking up space until then.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Can I use cloud storage instead of buying a bigger drive?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Often, yes, if you have reliable internet and don't mind paying for a cloud plan. A bigger drive makes more sense for large local files, games, and video editing.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Should I upgrade my drive or clean it up?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Clean up first. If you still run low after removing what you don't need, a larger SSD is a lasting fix.</span></p>
